Anthropic S-1: the short version

Filed October 26, 2026. Ticker “ANT” on Nasdaq. Boiled down from a 400-page filing.

The deal

A traditional IPO of Class A shares. Anthropic sells some, and existing holders sell some. Price, share count and raise size are all still blank, as normal for a first filing. Money goes to compute, hiring and general use. It also covers tax on employee RSU settlements.

Who’s who: the deal makers
Lead banks
Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley top the list. Then J.P. Morgan, Citigroup, Barclays and Wells Fargo. 22 banks in all, including BofA, Deutsche, RBC, UBS, Jefferies, Mizuho, MUFG and Cantor.
Lawyers
Wilson Sonsini Goodrich & Rosati for the company, with Anthropic’s in-house team (Brian Israel and others). Freshfields US is also named.
Founders
Seven co-founders: Dario Amodei (CEO), Daniela Amodei (President, board chair), Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Chris Olah. Together they hold one special Class F share through “Founder LLC”.
Long-Term Benefit Trust
Five financially disinterested trustees. They elect and can remove a majority of the board.
Amazon (AWS)
An April 2026 agreement lets Amazon buy up to $20B of convertible notes or shares, plus an option on up to $5B more in later rounds. The notes convert to non-voting Class N stock at IPO, up to an ownership cap.
Cloud partners
Claude is sold through AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure. Anthropic keeps the customer contract and pays each a fee. Chips come from Trainium, Google TPUs, NVIDIA and AMD.
Resellers and integrators
Accenture, Deloitte, KPMG, PwC, Salesforce and Snowflake. There is also a new services company, Ode with Anthropic, built with private equity firms.

  • Operations are close to break-even. The H1 operating loss was only 4% of revenue.
  • The huge net loss is mostly accounting. $72.4B of H1’s loss is a rise in the fair value of convertible notes and SAFEs as the valuation climbed. It is not cash spent.
  • 2025 included a $1.5B settlement (Bartz).
  • H1 sales and marketing jumped to $3.2B, from $120M a year earlier.
  • Revenue per employee is over $6.6M, up from $1.4M a year ago.
  • Total raised to date: $141.1B.
Customers and product
  • 11,900+ customers pay $100K or more a year. 1,900+ pay $1M or more. About 250 pay $10M or more.
  • Products: Claude Platform (API), Claude Code (coding agent) and Claude Cowork (knowledge-work agent). Cowork is being merged into Claude.ai.
  • Pricing is pay-per-use, metered in tokens. Opus 5.5 cut customer cost per task about 40% versus Opus 5.
  • The pitch: “Virtual Collaborators” that do hours- or days-long work autonomously.
Control: who really runs it
  • Public buyers get little say. The Class F share carries 50.1% of the vote. The Trust picks a board majority. Class A holders get one vote per share on the rest.
  • Anthropic is a public benefit corporation. Directors must balance shareholders, affected stakeholders and the mission.
  • It is a “controlled company” under Nasdaq rules, though it says it will lean on that exemption only for the nominating committee.
  • Amazon, other strategic partners and the 5% public-benefit stake hold non-voting Class N shares.
  • Public benefit shares: 5% of fully diluted shares went to a public benefit fund. The board may add up to 2% each time the stock rises 50% above the prior milestone. Each co-founder pledged 80% of their equity to philanthropy.
Top risks they flag
  • Compute: they compete with giant rivals, some of which are also their suppliers, for scarce chips and capacity. Commitments run to 2045 (amounts blank).
  • Customer concentration: a limited number of customers and distribution channels drive much of revenue.
  • Model behavior: models could act in unintended or misaligned ways, with liability and regulatory exposure.
  • Safety slowing releases: they may delay launches, as with Mythos and Project Glasswing, which could cost them ground.
  • Limited history: growth rates may not hold, and the company is young.
  • Self-improvement loop: models help build the next models, so flaws could compound.
Still blank in the filing: IPO price range, share counts, proceeds, Q3 2026 revenue (the prospectus shows a figure placeholder), and total compute commitments. Expect these in later amendments.
